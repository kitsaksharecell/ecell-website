Behind the Brand

Airbnb

Same Logo. A Different Story.

Every successful business begins with an idea, but not every idea begins with a business plan. Sometimes, it starts with a simple problem, a creative solution, and the courage to try something different.

The story of Airbnb is a remarkable example of how an ordinary challenge can become the foundation of a global business. What began with three friends trying to earn money to pay their rent eventually transformed the way people travel, stay, and experience destinations around the world.

📍 San Francisco (2008) 👥 Brian Chesky, Joe Gebbia & Nathan Blecharczyk 💡 Ordinary Challenge to Global Business ⏱️ 5 Min Read
💡

The Beginning of a Simple Idea

In 2008, three friends in San Francisco — Brian Chesky, Joe Gebbia, and Nathan Blecharczyk — were looking for a way to manage their financial challenges. When a design conference caused local hotels to fill up, they noticed an opportunity.

They decided to rent out air mattresses in their apartment to travelers who needed a place to stay. This small experiment was built around a simple observation: some people had extra space, while others needed affordable accommodation.

What appeared to be a temporary solution soon revealed the potential for something much bigger.

🚀

From Air Mattresses to a Global Platform

The founders recognized that their idea could address a wider need. Instead of limiting accommodation to traditional hotels, why not create a platform where people could offer their homes, rooms, and unique spaces to travelers?

With this vision, Airbnb began developing a new approach to hospitality. The platform connected hosts with guests, allowing people to discover accommodations that offered experiences beyond conventional hotel stays.

Over time, Airbnb expanded its reach and became a well-known global platform for finding and hosting unique places to stay.

“Its journey demonstrates that innovation does not always require inventing something entirely new. Sometimes, it means identifying existing resources and finding a better way to connect them with people who need them.”

Core Takeaways

The Entrepreneurial Lessons Behind Airbnb

The Airbnb story offers valuable lessons for aspiring entrepreneurs and students:

01
Identify Problems Around You

Many business opportunities begin with everyday challenges. The founders of Airbnb noticed a shortage of accommodation and explored a practical way to address it.

02
Start Small, Think Big

Airbnb began with a simple experiment involving air mattresses. Its story shows that a business does not need a complicated beginning to develop into an ambitious venture.

03
Innovation Comes from Different Perspectives

The founders looked at unused living spaces as potential accommodation. Their ability to view an ordinary situation differently helped them discover a new opportunity.

04
Create Value for Everyone

Airbnb created a platform that connected hosts seeking to share their spaces with travelers looking for accommodation. Building value for different groups can help businesses develop sustainable models.

05
Adapt and Grow with Changing Needs

Successful ventures must understand their users and evolve with their expectations. Airbnb's growth illustrates the importance of responding to changing customer preferences and opportunities.

🎓

What Students Can Learn from This Story

Practical insights for student founders & innovators

For students interested in entrepreneurship, Airbnb offers an important reminder: you do not need to begin with a perfect idea, a large investment, or a fully developed business model.

You can start by observing your surroundings, identifying a genuine problem, and exploring a solution using the resources available to you.

Whether it is developing a technology project, creating a campus solution, or addressing a community challenge, entrepreneurial thinking begins with curiosity and action.

The journey from a simple idea to a successful venture requires experimentation, learning, adaptability, and persistence.

Conclusion

Airbnb's journey from renting air mattresses in a San Francisco apartment to becoming a global hospitality platform highlights the power of innovative thinking and problem-solving.

Its story reminds us that great businesses are not always born from extraordinary beginnings — they often grow from ordinary problems viewed through extraordinary perspectives.

At the Entrepreneurship Cell, KITS Akshara Engineering College, we believe that every idea has the potential to create an impact when supported by creativity, determination, and the willingness to take the first step.

Your next big idea could be hiding in a simple problem you encounter today.

Behind the Brand

Nike

Same Logo. A Different Story.

Not every global empire starts with a grand vision. Sometimes, it starts with a coach who wanted better shoes for his runners, a student who believed in hustle, and a handshake deal that launched a revolution in athletic footwear.

The story of Nike is one of relentless innovation, bold branding, and the audacity to challenge an entire industry. What began as two men selling imported running shoes out of a car trunk eventually redefined sports, culture, and what it means to "Just Do It."

📍 Oregon, USA (1964) 👥 Phil Knight & Bill Bowerman 💡 From a Car Trunk to a Global Empire ⏱️ 5 Min Read
💡

The Beginning: Blue Ribbon Sports

In 1964, University of Oregon track coach Bill Bowerman and his former student Phil Knight founded a small company called Blue Ribbon Sports. Their initial idea was simple — import high-quality, low-cost running shoes from Onitsuka Tiger in Japan and sell them to American athletes.

Phil Knight sold shoes from the trunk of his car at track meets, while Bowerman experimented obsessively with shoe designs — even pouring rubber into his wife's waffle iron to create a lighter, grippier sole.

What started as a side hustle for two passionate runners soon revealed the potential for something far bigger than reselling imported sneakers.

🚀

From Waffle Irons to the Swoosh

By 1971, the partners decided to create their own brand. They named it Nike, after the Greek goddess of victory. A graphic design student, Carolyn Davidson, created the iconic Swoosh logo for just $35 — a symbol that would become one of the most recognized marks in the world.

Bowerman's relentless experimentation led to revolutionary shoe designs. His "Waffle Trainer" — inspired by the waffle iron experiment — became one of the best-selling training shoes in the United States and put Nike on the map as an innovator, not just a reseller.

By the late 1970s, Nike had overtaken Adidas in the U.S. running shoe market and was ready for its next giant leap.

"Nike's journey proves that innovation doesn't always mean inventing from scratch. Sometimes, it means looking at a waffle iron and seeing a running shoe — it means daring to reimagine what already exists."

Core Takeaways

The Entrepreneurial Lessons Behind Nike

The Nike story offers powerful lessons for aspiring entrepreneurs and students:

01
Start with What You Know

Phil Knight and Bill Bowerman didn't start in the shoe business — they started in running. Their deep understanding of athletes' needs gave them an unfair advantage. Build from your own expertise and passion.

02
Hustle Before You Scale

Knight sold shoes from a car trunk at track meets. There was no grand launch, no venture capital — just relentless hustle. Before thinking big, prove your idea works at the smallest possible scale.

03
Innovate Relentlessly

Bowerman's waffle iron experiment wasn't genius — it was obsession. He ruined countless prototypes before finding what worked. Innovation requires persistence, not just inspiration.

04
Build a Brand, Not Just a Product

Nike didn't just sell shoes — they sold a mindset. "Just Do It" became a universal call to action. Great brands stand for something bigger than what they sell.

05
Bet on People Who Believe in Your Vision

From signing an unknown Michael Jordan to backing Colin Kaepernick, Nike consistently invested in people who embodied their brand philosophy. Surround yourself with believers and give them a platform.

🎓

What Students Can Learn from This Story

Practical insights for student founders & innovators

For students exploring entrepreneurship, Nike's story is a masterclass in starting lean. Phil Knight didn't quit his accounting job until Blue Ribbon Sports was already profitable. He balanced a day job with his startup for years.

You don't need to drop everything to start something meaningful. Begin with your campus, your community, your network. Sell from a "car trunk" — whether that's a WhatsApp group, an Instagram page, or a college fest stall.

Bowerman's experiments remind us that the best innovations come from deeply understanding the people you serve. Talk to your users. Observe their pain points. Then iterate obsessively.

The Nike Swoosh was designed for $35. The waffle sole was born from a kitchen experiment. World-changing ideas don't need world-class budgets — they need world-class conviction.

Conclusion

Nike's journey from selling imported shoes out of a car trunk to becoming the world's most valuable sports brand is a testament to the power of passion, persistence, and bold storytelling.

Phil Knight and Bill Bowerman didn't wait for perfect conditions — they started with what they had, improved relentlessly, and built a brand that transcended sports to become a cultural movement.

At the Entrepreneurship Cell, KITS Akshara Engineering College, we believe that every student has the potential to build something extraordinary — not someday, but today, with the resources already at hand.

Your Swoosh moment is waiting. Just Do It.
